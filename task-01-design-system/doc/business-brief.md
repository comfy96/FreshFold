# Business brief

## The business
Fresh Fold Laundry is an owner-run neighbourhood laundry in Nigeria. The name is invented for this brief. The team collects clothes from customers' homes, washes and irons them, and returns them. Some customers bring clothes to the shop. Staff write names, phone numbers and clothes in a notebook. They count items at collection and again after ironing. The shop takes cash and bank transfers, and issues no receipts. The owner settles disputes.

## The person who uses it
Amaka Obi, 31, a bank officer. The customer age range is about 25 to 45.
- Device: an entry level Android phone with storage almost full.
- Connection: a small weekly data bundle, switched off between uses. Signal and power are unreliable.
- Skill: she uses WhatsApp and her bank app. She is not technical, and nobody is available to help her.
- Trigger: the shop has said nothing and she needs a shirt tomorrow, or she paid and wants proof.

## The one thing that must work
A customer sees the truth about her own order, from the shop's records, and never anyone else's. That means the item count, the status and the payment record, each dated.

## Three questions only this business can answer
1. Are my clothes ready for pickup?
2. How long will my clothes take?
3. Can I bring them to the shop, or can you collect them from me?

Only the shop's records hold these answers. If the records are silent, the app says so and names a person to contact.

## Why the interface must feel calm and plain
Customers worry about missing clothes and unrecorded money. A clever chatbot makes that worse, because she cannot tell whether to trust it. The interface must feel like a clear receipt book, not a chat.
- Her status shows first, before she types anything.
- Every answer shows the record underneath it and the date it was last updated.
- Words are short and plain. The app says "Our records show", never "You owe".
- When it cannot answer, it says so in one sentence and shows a named person.
- Screens stay light and work from saved data when the signal drops.

Trust comes from showing the record, and a cheap phone on small data needs a light, quiet screen.
